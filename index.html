ok, so why?

the manifesto

(yes, i know. a manifesto. very dramatic. bear with me.)

Remember when the web was weird? When somebody would spend a weekend making a little game or a silly calculator or a page that just shows a dancing hamster, and then just… put it up? Free. For anyone. No ten popups. No "we value your privacy" banner with 400 partners. No newsletter wall.

Somewhere along the way, everything turned into a funnel. Every page wants your email, your data, your attention, your subscription. Even the flashlight app wants to be a "platform".

This notebook is a tiny, stubborn "no."

indiethings is a hub for small independent things people built just because they wanted to. Games. Tools. Toys. Experiments. Half-finished ideas that are somehow still great. Each thing lives on its own, does its one job, and lets you leave when you're done.

"if it needs a loading spinner, a login, and a cookie banner just to tell me the time, it's not a tool, it's a toll booth."
— me, probably, at 2am
the ground rules

the rules

(more like vibes with numbers on them)

  1. Free. Actually free. Not "free trial". Not "free, but". Free like a library book — you walk in, you use it, you walk out.
  2. No ads. No trackers. No creepy stuff. Zero analytics scripts watching you click. If a thing needs to remember something, it stays in your browser.
  3. No accounts. You are not a "user". You are a person who wanted to play snake. Go ahead and play snake.
  4. Plain HTML, CSS and JS. webpack, npm install, 900MB of node_modules View source, and it actually makes sense. Right-click → learn something.
  5. One thing, done well (or at least done charmingly). Small is a feature. A thing should fit in your head and in a single folder.
  6. Works offline-ish, loads fast, respects your battery. If it's heavier than a photo of your cat, it's too heavy.
  7. Be weird. Be kind. Nobody is checking if it's a "viable business". Make the dumb little thing. Somebody out there needs exactly that.

your turn!

add yours

Made something small? Cool, i want it in here. The whole process is three steps, and none of them involve a "pipeline":

  1. Make a folder. things/your-thing/index.html
    Put whatever you want in it. Link ../../css/notebook.css if you want the paper look, or don't. Your thing, your rules (well, the rules up there).
  2. Add a line to the list. Open js/things.js and add an entry: slug, name, kind, a one-line description. Optionally a snarky note.
  3. Send it. Open a pull request, or just tell me about it. If it follows the rules, it goes in the notebook. Your name goes on it, in pen.

Not into git? Fine. Zip it, send it. We're not gatekeeping here.
